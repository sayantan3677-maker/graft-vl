# Commercial-use license audit: S3 training mix (hybrid_s3_lora_4b)

**Date:** 2026-11-22 · **Scope:** all 62 sources in `configs/train/hybrid_s3_lora_4b.json` (the release-candidate mix). That run starts from `exports/hybrid-q3-4b-aligned`, which was trained by `hybrid_align_4b.json`. The source sets in `hybrid_align_4b.json` and `hybrid_s2_lora_4b.json` are subsets of S3 (checked by script), so this table covers every dataset the released weights have seen in our pipeline. S1 sources (`textcaps`, `ureader_cap`) are **not** in the release lineage, because the hybrid uses Qwen3-VL-4B's vision tower.

> **This is not legal advice.** It is engineering research meant to brief counsel. Counsel must sign off before any product release. Class assignments are conservative. Where I could not confirm a license from a primary source, the row is **B**, never A.

## Method and legend

- I traced each source from its FineVision subset (`src/vlm/data/sources.py`) back to the **original upstream** dataset. FineVision's card gives no license of its own: "Each of the publicly available sub-datasets ... are governed by specific licensing conditions" ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceM4/FineVision)). I then read the upstream LICENSE file, dataset card, project page or paper.
- **A**: the annotations are permissively licensed (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, MIT, Apache, ODC-BY, CDLA-P) **and** the images are synthetic or permissively licensed.
- **B**: the annotations are permissive or unknown, but the images are third-party (Flickr/COCO, LAION, web crawl, scanned docs) or the license could not be confirmed.
- **C**: restricted. The reason tags are:
  - **NC**: an explicit non-commercial, research-only or evaluation-only license.
  - **REG**: distributed only after registration on the ICDAR Robust Reading Competition (RRC) portal, with third-party images.
  - **GEN**: the targets were generated by a proprietary model whose terms restrict using its output to build competing models (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google).
  - **INHERIT**: a repackaging or derivative of other C sources.
- **GEN** is a contractual (terms-of-service) risk, not a copyright one. Counsel may treat some GEN rows as B, especially the indirect ones: `densefusion`, `unichart` and `pixmo_cap`. This audit keeps them as C, per the brief.
- **Shares** are the `weight` values in the S3 config. They sum to 1.0 across all 62 entries, including the 18% of text-only replay data.
- **Caveat:** the RRC portal (`rrc.cvc.uab.es`) could not be fetched because of a TLS certificate error. REG rows therefore rest on the registration-gated download plus the image provenance. Counsel should read the RRC terms directly.

### Surprises

- **olmOCR-mix-0225 is GPT-4o output.** Its card says the transcriptions were made by `gpt-4o-2024-08-06` and are "subject to OpenAI's terms of use". Its ODC-BY tag does not cover the generator's terms.
- **CoSyn-400K is not "clean synthetic".** The card says its images come from Claude-generated code under Anthropic's terms, and its questions were written by GPT-4o-mini under OpenAI's terms. Together the three subsets are 6.5% of the mix.
- **PixMo-Cap carries an Anthropic-terms notice** (Claude was used to process the transcripts). Its images are also unlicensed web URLs that we fetched ourselves.
- **ChartQA is GPL-3.0, not research-only** as `LICENSES.md` states. Its images are still crawled from Statista, Pew, OWID and OECD, so it stays B. The GPL's effect on model weights is an open question for counsel.
- **IconQA's README contradicts itself.** The badge says CC BY-SA 4.0, but the license text says CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. I took the stricter reading.
- **The in-house sets are not clean:**
  - `synth-reading-200k` uses `pixmo_cap` web photos as backgrounds and tulu-3 text.
  - The reasoning traces reuse DocVQA, InfoVQA, ScienceQA and IconQA images.
  - The text replay prompts come from `allenai/tulu-3-sft-mixture`, whose card says "Some portions of the dataset are non-commercial". `gen_replay.py` explicitly samples `no_robots`, which is CC BY-NC.
- **`LICENSES.md` is out of date on the backbone.** It lists SigLIP2 as the vision tower, but the release uses the Qwen3-VL-4B vision encoder (Apache-2.0 weights; its training data is undisclosed). This needs a row of its own.

## Recommendation for a "commercial-safe" retrain

These recommendations are for counsel to confirm.

**1. Drop all C sources (46.1%).** Renormalize the remaining weights, and retrain alignment as well as S3: the aligned checkpoint has already seen C data, so a clean product model must start again from `exports/hybrid-q3-4b-vit4b`.

**2. Decide B by policy. Two options:**
- **Strict (A only):** this leaves about 3% of the current mix, which is not viable without new data.
- **Pragmatic (A plus low-risk B):** keep B sources whose annotations are CC BY or Apache and whose images are widely used by industry:
  - COCO / Open Images / Flickr: `vqav2`, `aokvqa`, `tallyqa`, `vsr`, `visual7w`, `localized_narratives`, `lnqa`, `textvqa`, `cocotext`
  - RICO screenshots: `screenqa`, `screen2words`
  - Public filings and government tables: `tat_qa`, `tat_dqa`, `hitab`, `ureader_ie`
  - Open-licensed annotations with web-PDF images: `doclingmatix`
  - Synthetic images: `figureqa` (after its in-archive license is checked)

  Pragmatic mode should still exclude these higher-risk B sources until counsel clears them:
  - crawled Statista charts: `chartqa`, `chart2text`, `vistext`
  - no license at all: `visualmrc`, `pdfvqa`, `invoices_receipts`, `rendered_text`
  - web-scraped diagrams: `ai2d`

**3. Fix the in-house sets cheaply. They can become A:**
- Re-render `synth-reading-200k` with clean backgrounds (procedural textures, or CC0 photos from Unsplash or Pexels under their licenses) and with text from a permissive corpus (Wikipedia CC BY-SA, or tulu subsets filtered to ODC-BY or Apache).
- Rebuild the replay sets after excluding `no_robots` and any other NC tulu subsets. Qwen3 responses are Apache-2.0.
- Regenerate the traces using only A or cleared-B images.

**Capabilities that would weaken:**
- **Document QA and OCR (largest hit).** DocVQA, InfoVQA, UReader, olmOCR, CoSyn-doc, SlideVQA and SROIE are all removed, which takes out 19.5 of the 25.4 points of document weight. Expect large drops on DocVQA, InfoVQA and OCRBench, which are already our weakest benchmarks.
- **Charts.** UniChart, CoSyn-chart, DVQA and MMC go, and ChartQA/Chart2Text go too in pragmatic mode. Only PlotQA, MapQA, RobuT, FigureQA and HiTab would remain, all synthetic or templated. Real-world chart style will degrade.
- **Scene-text and handwriting OCR.** ST-VQA, OCR-VQA, LLaVAR, TextOCR-GPT4V and IAM go. TextVQA and COCO-Text remain in pragmatic mode. Handwriting would have no source left at all.
- **Science and diagrams.** ScienceQA, TQA, IconQA, Geo170K and ArxivQA go, and AI2D is at risk.
- **Dense captioning and web QA.** PixMo-Cap, DenseFusion and VisualWebInstruct go.

**Known permissive replacements (verify each license before use):**

| Gap | Candidate | Declared license / basis |
|---|---|---|
| Documents (synthetic) | In-house renderer extending `synth-reading` with Docling/HTML-to-PDF layouts, plus QA from Apache-licensed Qwen models; SynthDoG (Donut) | We own the output; SynthDoG code MIT |

| Documents (real) | Docmatix (PDFA) | MIT annotations; images B |
| Documents (real) | IDL-WDS / PDFA OCR with own transcription by an open model | Image rights B |
| Charts and tables | Regenerate CoSyn-style data with an **open-weights** code LLM (e.g. Qwen2.5/3-Coder, Apache-2.0) using the CoSyn pipeline code | Pipeline code Apache-2.0 |
| Charts and tables | PlotQA, MapQA, RobuT (already A); FigureQA once confirmed; ChartGemma-style data regenerated with open models; charts rendered from OWID data | OWID charts and data are CC BY 4.0 |
| OCR and scene text | SynthText / TextRecognitionDataGenerator-style renders, with fonts restricted to OFL/Apache | Generator code permissive |
| OCR and scene text | Re-caption TextOCR / Open Images with an open model in place of GPT-4V | Image rights B |
| Handwriting | Synthetic handwriting from OFL handwriting fonts (RenderedText approach, re-rendered by us with vetted fonts) | We own the output |
| Captions and VQA | Re-caption permissively licensed images (Open Images, CC BY 2.0 subset; Wikimedia Commons CC0/CC BY) with Qwen3-VL (Apache-2.0) | Image rights mostly B-low |

| UI | Rendered HTML from open-model-generated code (WebSight pipeline) | We own the output |

The strongest commercial path is to **regenerate the synthetic sets ourselves with open-weights (Apache/MIT) generators**: CoSyn-style charts, tables and documents, WebSight-style UI, and synth-reading. Synthetic data generated in-house carries no third-party image rights and no proprietary-model terms, and it targets exactly the capabilities that dropping C removes.

## Shared upstream terms referenced above

- **MS-COCO** ([terms](https://cocodataset.org/#termsofuse)): the annotations are CC BY 4.0. "The COCO Consortium does not own the copyright of the images", and use of the images must follow Flickr's terms. Per-image licenses on Flickr include NC variants.
- **Open Images** ([page](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html)): "The annotations are licensed by Google LLC under CC BY 4.0 license. The images are listed as having a CC BY 2.0 license. Note: while we tried to identify images that are licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution license, we make no representations or warranties..."
- **LAION** (`llavar`, `densefusion`): LAION distributes URLs only. The images belong to third parties.
- **Generator terms** (GEN): OpenAI's terms prohibit using output "to develop models that compete with OpenAI". Anthropic's Commercial Terms restrict using the services to build competing AI models. The Gemini API terms contain similar restrictions. Whether these clauses bind downstream users of a third-party dataset, as opposed to only the party that generated it, is the key question for counsel.
